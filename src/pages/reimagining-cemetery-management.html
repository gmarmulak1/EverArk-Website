REIMAGINING CEMETERY MANAGEMENT

For more than 200 years, cemeteries have struggled with paper maps, keeping accurate written records, storing hard copies of documents, and ensuring inventory is up to date. 
​
Cemeteries everywhere are ending these archaic administrative practices by switching to digital management systems. In this day and age, when cemeteries fail to utilize this technology, they may be inviting disaster and putting their cemetery’s reputation on the line.

Shoddy Cemetery Management Leads to Shock and Heartache

In 2008, Arlington National Cemetery came under scrutiny when the widow of an army soldier complained that the wrong headstone was sitting atop her husband’s grave. The Pentagon got involved and their investigation revealed many more problems. Word traveled fast and soon numerous people and organizations started investigating the tragic circumstances at Arlington Cemetery.

To everyone’s dismay, investigators discovered that two servicemen had been buried in the same grave, coffins had been placed in shallow graves, and a servicemember’s cremated remains were buried in an occupied grave that was unmarked. In other cases, grave markers were not placed on graves immediately following burials and records were not updated, which resulted in questions surrounding the identities of decedents. Other heartbreaking instances include gravediggers discovering a casket while they were excavating a “vacant” plot and burial urns that had been unearthed, then dumped into a landfill. 

In May of 2009, the inspector general’s report was complete. It revealed that cemetery officials were negligent because they continued to use a paper filing system to keep track of cemetery operations instead of the computerized database they had at their disposal.   

Investigations continued and more problems - from mismanaged records to discarded headstones – were discovered. More than two years later, the Northern Virginia Technology Council concluded its three-month investigation into the cemetery’s administrative practices. Their report stated, “the cemetery must digitize its records, improve its scheduling system, and establish a rigorous chain of custody for remains as they move from funeral homes to burial.”
